# E0: EEGNet on BNCI2014-001, the pipeline check (development numbers)

**Date:** Sat 3 Oct 2026 (the Day-3 plan, Mon 5 – Tue 6 Oct, done early).
**Status:** development numbers; not for the YC application as headline numbers.
**Verdict: ✅ E0 passes, pending one check.** EEGNet + EA scores **75.0%** against the literature's **74.3% ± 3** (pass band 71.3–77.3%), on our own causal 2-s pipeline.

⚠️ **4 Oct:** the on-screen cue alone is decodable at 75.8% (diagnostic 1 below). A post-cue window (diagnostic 3) must confirm that the 75.0% is motor imagery before it's quoted anywhere.

## The question
Architecture §8.5, E0: *does our pipeline reproduce the literature?*
- **Target:** EA + EEGNet + SWA ≈ 74.3% on BNCI2014-001, left vs right hand, leave-one-subject-out over 9 people ([Sagayaraju 2026](../research/papers/2026-sagayaraju-ea-swa-calibration-bottleneck.md): 74.31% ± 10.94).
- **Go/no-go:** within ±3 points. Otherwise it's a pipeline bug, and work stops until it's found.

## Protocol
- **Data:** BNCI2014-001, all 9 people (none is in the locked test set, D-026), left vs right hand, both sessions: **288 windows per person** (144 per class).
- **Signal, our product contract (§4.2), not the paper's:**
  - causal 4–30 Hz band-pass + notches at 250 Hz, then 128 Hz;
  - **2-s windows** at [cue + 0.5 s, cue + 2.5 s];
  - all 22 EEG channels, reference as recorded.
  - The paper used 1–40 Hz, removed eye artefacts with ICA, and most likely used 4-s windows.
- **Alignment:** EA with **one reference per recording session** (D-029), label-free. Like the paper, the reference uses the whole session (offline). E2 measures the online, growing version.
- **Model:** EEGNet (F1/D/F2 = 16/2/32, 64-sample kernel, dropout 0.5) + 64-d embedding + linear head: **20,002 weights** (D-029).
- **Training, the paper's recipe:**
  - AdamW, learning rate 1e-3 on a cosine to 0, weight decay 0.05, **300 epochs**;
  - **SWA over epochs 226–300**;
  - batches of 8 people × 8 windows.
  - Each fold trains on all 8 other people (no validation people, no early stopping), and each person is tested once.
- **Seed:** one (20261005), for weights, dropout and batch order.
- **Statistics:** balanced accuracy (chance 50%), mean ± SD over people, 95% bootstrap CI over people, paired Wilcoxon over people (§8.3).
- **Compute:** the dev laptop's CPU, 9 folds in parallel (2 threads each): **36 min** with EA, **28 min** without.

## What it means
1. **The pipeline is sound.**
   - We reproduce the paper's number (75.0 vs 74.3) and its pattern: people 2 and 5 hardest, 3 among the best.
   - We did it with the pipeline we ship: causal filters, 2-s windows, no ICA.
   - So harmonisation, labels, epoch timing, filters, EA, the model and the trainer all work together.
   - **The 4-s-window run wasn't needed for the verdict.** It was there to tell a bug from a protocol difference if the 2-s run fell short. It ran anyway on 4 Oct: 83.7%, but that number probably includes decoding the cue itself (see the next section).
2. **The deep model is worth its complexity:** +8.1 points over the best Riemannian baseline with the same alignment (p = 0.039, better for 7 of 9 people). The Day-2 results set a bar of about 5.
3. **Alignment helps EEGNet too, but less than it helps MDM.** EEGNet gains +2.9 points (better for 8 of 9 people, p = 0.074: suggestive, not significant with 9 people; person 6 loses 4.9), against +12 for MDM. EEGNet's batch norm already absorbs part of each person's scale; EA removes the rest of the person-specific mixing.
4. **An observation, not a claim.**
   - On these 9 people, the zero-calibration model (75.0%) is indistinguishable from a Riemannian model *calibrated* on each person's own first session (75.8%; −0.9 points, p = 0.59). It's equal or better for 5 of 9 people, including the hard ones (2, 4, 5, 6).
   - The comparison flatters us: n = 9, EA here sees each test session in full, and the calibrated baseline is a simple Riemannian model, not a calibrated deep model.
   - Still, it's the first sign of the gap NeurOS is built to close. E2 measures how much of it survives a growing online reference.
5. **Some people stay hard:** 5 of 9 people are below 70%. Calibration doesn't save person 2 either (55.6% within-person). This is "BCI inefficiency", and it's why the headline reports the per-person distribution and the share of people ≥ 70%, not only the mean.

## The paper's 4-s window (run 4 Oct, 00:40): 83.7%, but probably not all motor imagery
The same experiment (EEGNet + EA, same recipe and seed) with the paper's window **[cue, cue + 4 s]** instead of ours, [cue + 0.5, cue + 2.5 s].

| Person | 2-s window (ours) | 4-s window (paper's) | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 87.2 | 91.0 | +3.8 |
| 2 | 60.4 | 77.1 | **+16.7** |
| 3 | 89.2 | 85.4 | −3.8 |
| 4 | 68.4 | 78.1 | +9.7 |
| 5 | 65.3 | 82.3 | **+17.0** |
| 6 | 67.0 | 78.5 | +11.5 |
| 7 | 66.7 | 86.8 | **+20.1** |
| 8 | 89.6 | 93.4 | +3.8 |
| 9 | 80.9 | 80.6 | −0.3 |
| **Mean ± SD** | **75.0 ± 11.6** | **83.7 ± 5.8** | **+8.7** (p = 0.031, better for 7 / 9) |

- 95% CI [80.2, 87.4]; all 9 people ≥ 70%; κ 0.674.
- 36,386 weights (the longer window adds inputs to the embedding layer); 72 min on the laptop.

**Don't use this number yet. Three things don't add up:**
1. **It beats the paper by 9.4 points with the same window.** The paper removed eye-movement components with ICA; we didn't.
2. **The gain is concentrated in the people who were hardest:** persons 2, 5 and 7 gain 17–20 points.
   - Person 2 now scores 77% *across people*, yet only 55.6% with a model calibrated on their own data.
   - Their motor imagery is unlikely to have become that much more readable.
3. **The 4-s window starts the instant the arrow appears.**
   - BNCI2014-001 shows an arrow pointing left or right for 1.25 s from the cue.
   - The brain's visual response to it, and any glance toward it, differ by class. Frontal channels pick up eye movements, and the 4–30 Hz band-pass doesn't remove all of that.
   - Our contract starts windows at cue + 0.5 s precisely to skip the response to the cue (§4.2). None of this exists in real use: there's no arrow.

**Most likely reading:**
- Part of the +8.7 is genuine: longer windows carry more motor-imagery signal, which is well documented.
- Part is probably the model reading the cue itself.
- This run alone can't separate the two.

**How to separate them** (two runs, ≈ 1 h 15 min on the laptop, or minutes on a GPU):
- **Cue-only, [cue, cue + 0.5 s].** Motor imagery barely starts within half a second, so accuracy well above chance here means the cue itself is decodable.
- **Long but cue-free, [cue + 0.5, cue + 4.0 s] (3.5 s).**
  - If it keeps most of the gain, the gain is window length.
  - If it falls back toward 75%, the gain came from the first half-second.

### Diagnostic 1 (4 Oct, 12:07): the cue alone is decodable, at 75.8%
Same model and recipe, trained and tested on **[cue, cue + 0.5 s] only**: the first half-second after the arrow appears, before motor imagery has time to change the EEG. 64 samples per window; 7,714 weights. Code `f87b0d8`, clean.

| Person | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 | Mean ± SD |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cue only, [0, 0.5 s] | 77.8 | 78.8 | 70.5 | 77.4 | 78.5 | 78.1 | 83.3 | 72.6 | 65.3 | **75.8 ± 5.4** (CI [72.3, 78.9]; 8 / 9 people ≥ 70%) |
| Our 2-s window, [0.5, 2.5 s] | 87.2 | 60.4 | 89.2 | 68.4 | 65.3 | 67.0 | 66.7 | 89.6 | 80.9 | 75.0 ± 11.6 |

**What it shows:**
- **Left vs right can be decoded from the cue itself as well as from our motor-imagery window.** And it works for nearly everyone, including the people whose motor imagery is hard to decode (persons 2, 5 and 7: 78–83% here, 60–67% on imagery).
- **This is known neuroscience.** A central arrow shifts attention to its side and evokes lateralised brain responses within a few hundred milliseconds, plus any glances toward it. None of this exists in real use, where there is no arrow.
- **The 4-s window's 83.7% is therefore largely cue-reading, not motor imagery. Never quote it.**
- **It also raises a question about our own 75.0%.** Our window starts 0.5 s after the cue, but the arrow stays on screen until 1.25 s, and attention-related responses can last that long.
- **The clean test is diagnostic 3** below, a window that starts after the arrow is gone. The paper we compared against also included the cue's onset in its window (it removed eye movements with ICA, but not attention responses), so its 74.3% may carry the same inflation.

### Diagnostics 2 and 3: pending

  - started 4 Oct 12:07;

  - No result. To re-run on mains power (≈ 65 min).
- **Diagnostic 3, post-cue 2-s window, [1.5, 3.5 s]** (`configs/e0_post_cue.yaml`):
  - the decisive test for our E0 number; not started yet (≈ 36 min on mains power).
  - If it stays near 75%, E0 stands as a motor-imagery result. If it drops, E0's 75.0% was partly cue-driven, and E0 has to be re-judged on this window.

**Why it matters beyond E0:**
- Every training dataset we use is cued, so the evaluation must not reward decoding the cue. A model that leans on it scores well offline and worse live.
- The 2-s product window already skips the cue's onset. The arrow is still on screen for its first 0.75 s, though, so E0b should also check a window that starts after the arrow disappears (cue + 1.25 s).

## Caveats
- **One seed.** Deep-network results move by about a point between seeds. 75.0 sits 3.7 points inside the band's lower edge, so the verdict doesn't hinge on the seed. More seeds belong on the cluster (Q6b).
- **9 people:** confidence intervals are ±7 points wide. These numbers check the pipeline; they prove nothing about 380 people.
- **Offline, full-session alignment:** each test session's EA reference uses the whole session (unlabeled). The product gets a growing stream; E2 measures the cost.
- **Development, not headline:** BNCI2014-001 is a literature-comparison dataset used during development (D-026), never part of the locked test set.
