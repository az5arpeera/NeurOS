# E0: EEGNet on BNCI2014-001, the pipeline check (development numbers)

**Date:** Sat 3 Oct 2026 (the Day-3 plan, Mon 5 – Tue 6 Oct, done early); cue diagnostics Sun 4 Oct.
**Status:** development numbers; not for the YC application as headline numbers.

**Verdict (revised 4 Oct, after the cue diagnostics):**
- **✅ The pipeline check passes.**
  - EEGNet + EA scores **75.0%** on our causal 2-s window, against the literature's **74.3% ± 3** (pass band 71.3–77.3%).
  - A window that ends at the cue scores at chance (47–51% for every model). Nothing leaks the class, and the epoch timing is right.
- **❌ But 75.0% is not a zero-calibration motor-imagery number. Never quote it.**
  - Our window starts 0.5 s after the cue, while the arrow is still on screen (until 1.25 s).
  - In a window that starts after the arrow has gone, **the same model scores 60.6%** (diagnostic 3).
  - That's level with the simplest cross-person Riemannian model (60.4%), and **11.6 points below a model calibrated on each person's own data** (72.2%; p = 0.012, worse for 8 of 9 people).
- **E0's cue-controlled zero-calibration reference is 60.6% ± 7.9** (9 people, one seed).
- **The 4-s window's 83.7% is retracted too.** Without the arrow's first half-second, a 3.5-s window scores 72.3%, no better than our 2-s window (diagnostic 2).

## The cue diagnostics at a glance (4 Oct)
Balanced accuracy %, mean over the 9 people, same recipe and seed. Windows are in seconds from the cue: the arrow is on screen from 0 to 1.25 s, and imagery continues until 4 s.

| Window | What it contains | EEGNet + EA (zero-calibration) | TS+LR + EA (zero-calibration) | TS+LR calibrated on the person's session 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [−0.5, 0] | the fixation cross: nothing to decode yet | | 48.0 | 49.8 |
| [0, 0.5] | the arrow's onset | 75.8 | | |
| **[0.5, 2.5]**, our contract (E0) | the arrow until 1.25 s, then imagery | **75.0** | 66.9 | 75.8 |
| **[1.5, 3.5]** | imagery only; the arrow has gone | **60.6** | 60.4 | 72.2 |
| [0.5, 4.0] | the arrow until 1.25 s, then imagery | 72.3 | | |
| [0, 4], the paper's | the arrow's onset, then imagery | 83.7 | | |

**How to read it:**
- **The imagery signal is still there after the arrow has gone.** The calibrated model loses only 3.6 points (p = 0.11).
- **The zero-calibration EEGNet loses 14.4** (p = 0.004, all 9 people): 10.7 points more than the calibrated model (p = 0.012, 8 of 9 people).
- **EEGNet's lead over the Riemannian model disappears:** +8.1 points on our window (p = 0.039), +0.2 after the arrow (p = 1.0).
- **So what EEGNet reads *across people* in our window sits in its first second, while the arrow is still on screen.**
  - It has to be activity that looks alike in everyone and is locked to the cue's timing. EEGNet reads waveform shape and timing; covariance-based models mostly don't, which is why it led there.
  - The brain's response to the arrow fits: on its own, it decodes at 75.8%.
  - For the best performers, the onset of imagery right after the cue may contribute too. Their scores on our window are the ones the arrow explains least (correlation −0.64 between the 9 people's scores on our window and on the arrow's onset; suggestive with n = 9).
  - Neither has a fixed time in real use: there's no arrow, and imagery starts whenever the user starts it.

## The question
Architecture §8.5, E0: *does our pipeline reproduce the literature?*
- **Target:** EA + EEGNet + SWA ≈ 74.3% on BNCI2014-001, left vs right hand, leave-one-subject-out over 9 people ([Sagayaraju 2026](../research/papers/2026-sagayaraju-ea-swa-calibration-bottleneck.md): 74.31% ± 10.94).
- **Go/no-go:** within ±3 points. Otherwise it's a pipeline bug, and work stops until it's found.

## Protocol
- **Data:** BNCI2014-001, all 9 people (none is in the locked test set, D-026), left vs right hand, both sessions: **288 windows per person** (144 per class).
- **Signal, our product contract (§4.2), not the paper's:**
  - causal 4–30 Hz band-pass + notches at 250 Hz, then 128 Hz;
  - **2-s windows** at [cue + 0.5 s, cue + 2.5 s];
  - all 22 EEG channels, reference as recorded.
  - The paper used 1–40 Hz, removed eye artefacts with ICA, and most likely used 4-s windows.
- **Alignment:** EA with **one reference per recording session** (D-029), label-free. Like the paper, the reference uses the whole session (offline). E2 measures the online, growing version.
- **Model:** EEGNet (F1/D/F2 = 16/2/32, 64-sample kernel, dropout 0.5) + 64-d embedding + linear head: **20,002 weights** (D-029).
- **Training, the paper's recipe:**
  - AdamW, learning rate 1e-3 on a cosine to 0, weight decay 0.05, **300 epochs**;
  - **SWA over epochs 226–300**;
  - batches of 8 people × 8 windows.
  - Each fold trains on all 8 other people (no validation people, no early stopping), and each person is tested once.
- **Seed:** one (20261005), for weights, dropout and batch order.
- **Statistics:** balanced accuracy (chance 50%), mean ± SD over people, 95% bootstrap CI over people, paired Wilcoxon over people (§8.3).
- **Compute:** the dev laptop's CPU, 9 folds in parallel (2 threads each): **36 min** with EA, **28 min** without.

## What it means (revised 4 Oct)
1. **The pipeline is sound.**
   - We reproduce the paper's number (75.0 vs 74.3) and its pattern: people 2 and 5 hardest, 3 among the best.
   - We did it with the pipeline we ship: causal filters, 2-s windows, no ICA.
   - So harmonisation, labels, epoch timing, filters, EA, the model and the trainer all work together.
   - The cue checks agree: chance before the cue, 75.8% from the arrow's first half-second. The event timing is where we think it is.
2. **The deep model's lead is not shown.** EEGNet + EA beat the best Riemannian model by 8.1 points on our window (p = 0.039). After the arrow has gone, it leads by 0.2. The deep-vs-Riemannian question goes back to E1, on cue-controlled windows and many more people.
3. **Alignment helps.**
   - EA added 2.9 points to EEGNet on our window (better for 8 of 9 people, p = 0.074: suggestive, not significant with 9 people). That pair wasn't re-run after the arrow.
   - After the arrow, EA still helps the Riemannian models: TS+LR +3.0 (p = 0.105), MDM +7.8 (p = 0.008).
4. **Retracted 4 Oct: "zero-calibration matches a calibrated model".**
   - On our window, the zero-calibration model (75.0%) looked indistinguishable from a Riemannian model calibrated on each person's own first session (75.8%; p = 0.59).
   - After the arrow has gone, the gap is **11.6 points** (60.6 vs 72.2; p = 0.012, worse for 8 of 9 people).
   - That gap is what NeurOS exists to close, now measured without the cue.
5. **Some people stay hard:** 5 of 9 people are below 70% on our window, and calibration doesn't save person 2 either (55.6% within-person). This is "BCI inefficiency", and it's why the headline reports the per-person distribution and the share of people ≥ 70%, not only the mean.

## What this means for NeurOS
1. **The calibration gap is real: about 12 points on this dataset.**
   - Zero-calibration 60.6% vs calibrated 72.2%, with the cue controlled.
   - It's the gap the adaptation layer (L1) and E2 have to close.
   - It's also the honest story for YC: not "already on par", but "we measured the gap, and here's how we close it".
2. **Cross-person evaluation on cued data rewards reading the cue.**
   - The response to the arrow is much the same in everyone (75.8% across people, from half a second of EEG). Motor imagery isn't.
   - So a zero-calibration model learns the cue first, and windows that include the cue period overstate it.
   - Published cross-subject numbers measured on such windows may be inflated the same way. That includes the 74.3% we matched, and possibly the "77–83% left/right" range in our [synthesis](../research/synthesis.md). Check each source's window before leaning on its number.
3. **Protocol change, adopted 4 Oct ([D-030](../decisions/decision-log.md)):**
   - every motor-imagery result uses windows that start after the class has left the screen (training too), next to a cue-only control;
   - on BNCI2014-001 that's [1.5, 3.5] s, so **E0's result under the current contract is diagnostic 3's 60.6%**;
   - the five core datasets all keep the class on screen for the whole task (a target, an arrow or classifier feedback), so their results always carry the cue-only control and a flag;
   - the headline target (§8.6: X ≈ 75% pooled, set from cue-period numbers) is revisited after E1.
4. **It's a credibility asset.** We found the confound in our own best number within a day, controlled it, and report the lower figure. That's the "published, protocol-controlled evidence" edge in the [competitor analysis](../business/competitors.md).

## The paper's 4-s window (run 4 Oct, 00:40): 83.7%, retracted as cue-reading
The same experiment (EEGNet + EA, same recipe and seed) with the paper's window **[cue, cue + 4 s]** instead of ours, [cue + 0.5, cue + 2.5 s].

| Person | 2-s window (ours) | 4-s window (paper's) | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 87.2 | 91.0 | +3.8 |
| 2 | 60.4 | 77.1 | **+16.7** |
| 3 | 89.2 | 85.4 | −3.8 |
| 4 | 68.4 | 78.1 | +9.7 |
| 5 | 65.3 | 82.3 | **+17.0** |
| 6 | 67.0 | 78.5 | +11.5 |
| 7 | 66.7 | 86.8 | **+20.1** |
| 8 | 89.6 | 93.4 | +3.8 |
| 9 | 80.9 | 80.6 | −0.3 |
| **Mean ± SD** | **75.0 ± 11.6** | **83.7 ± 5.8** | **+8.7** (p = 0.031, better for 7 / 9) |

- 95% CI [80.2, 87.4]; all 9 people ≥ 70%; κ 0.674.
- 36,386 weights (the longer window adds inputs to the embedding layer); 72 min on the laptop.

**Why it was flagged straight away:**
1. **It beats the paper by 9.4 points with the same window.** The paper removed eye-movement components with ICA; we didn't.
2. **The gain is concentrated in the people who were hardest:** persons 2, 5 and 7 gain 17–20 points.
   - Person 2 scores 77% *across people*, yet only 55.6% with a model calibrated on their own data.
   - Their motor imagery is unlikely to have become that much more readable.
3. **The 4-s window starts the instant the arrow appears.**
   - BNCI2014-001 shows an arrow pointing left or right for 1.25 s from the cue.
   - The brain's visual response to it, and any glance toward it, differ by class. Frontal channels pick up eye movements, and the 4–30 Hz band-pass doesn't remove all of that.
   - Our contract starts windows at cue + 0.5 s to skip the response to the cue's onset (§4.2). None of this exists in real use: there's no arrow.

Three diagnostics followed: the cue only, a long window without the cue's onset, and a window after the arrow has gone.

### Diagnostic 1 (4 Oct, 12:07): the cue alone is decodable, at 75.8%
Same model and recipe, trained and tested on **[cue, cue + 0.5 s] only**: the first half-second after the arrow appears, before motor imagery has time to change the EEG. 64 samples per window; 7,714 weights. Code `f87b0d8`, clean.

| Person | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 | Mean ± SD |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cue only, [0, 0.5 s] | 77.8 | 78.8 | 70.5 | 77.4 | 78.5 | 78.1 | 83.3 | 72.6 | 65.3 | **75.8 ± 5.4** (CI [72.3, 78.9]; 8 / 9 people ≥ 70%) |
| Our 2-s window, [0.5, 2.5 s] | 87.2 | 60.4 | 89.2 | 68.4 | 65.3 | 67.0 | 66.7 | 89.6 | 80.9 | 75.0 ± 11.6 |

**What it shows:**
- **Left vs right can be decoded from the cue itself as well as from our motor-imagery window.** It works for nearly everyone, including the people whose motor imagery is hard to decode (persons 2, 5 and 7: 78–83% here, 60–67% on imagery).
- **This is known neuroscience.** A central arrow shifts attention to its side and evokes lateralised brain responses within a few hundred milliseconds, plus any glances toward it. None of this exists in real use, where there is no arrow.
- **The 4-s window's 83.7% is therefore largely cue-reading, not motor imagery. Never quote it.**
- It raised the question diagnostic 3 answers: our window starts 0.5 s after the cue, but the arrow stays on screen until 1.25 s.

**What else the run shows:**
- **6 of 9 people are still above chance on their own** (the 95% chance band for 288 windows is 44.1–55.9%). Persons 2, 5 and 9 are at chance.
- **The model fits its training people less well here too.** Final training loss is 0.26–0.31, against 0.12–0.18 on our window. So this window holds less class information that's shared across people; it isn't only a test-time effect.
- **The ranking of people carries over** (correlation 0.74 with our window). The drop is broad, not a few people collapsing.
- **Person 9 is the starkest:** 80.9 → 53.1 (chance) across people, while a model calibrated on their own data keeps 84.0.

### Diagnostic 2 (4 Oct, 16:02): without the arrow's onset, the 4-s gain disappears
Same model, recipe and seed, on the **long window without the cue's onset, [cue + 0.5, cue + 4.0 s]**: the arrow's last 0.75 s, then 2.75 s of imagery. 448 samples per window, 32,290 weights, 52 min. Code `cdde893`, clean. (A first attempt at 12:07 stopped at epoch 100 of 300: the laptop was on battery, throttled to about a quarter of its speed, and the job hit the 2-hour limit.)

| Person | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 | Mean ± SD |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Without the onset, [0.5, 4.0]** | 82.3 | 60.4 | 88.5 | 56.9 | 66.7 | 63.5 | 68.1 | 88.5 | 76.0 | **72.3 ± 12.0** (CI [65.2, 80.0]; 4 / 9 people ≥ 70%; κ 0.447) |
| The paper's 4-s window, [0, 4] | 91.0 | 77.1 | 85.4 | 78.1 | 82.3 | 78.5 | 86.8 | 93.4 | 80.6 | 83.7 ± 5.8 |
| Our 2-s window, [0.5, 2.5] | 87.2 | 60.4 | 89.2 | 68.4 | 65.3 | 67.0 | 66.7 | 89.6 | 80.9 | 75.0 ± 11.6 |

**What it shows:**
- **The 4-s window's gain was the arrow's onset, not the window's length.**
  - Dropping its first half-second takes the score from 83.7 to 72.3 (−11.3 points, p = 0.008, lower for 8 of 9 people).
  - The resulting 3.5-s window is no better than our 2-s one (−2.6, p = 0.13, higher for only 2 of 9).
  - The hardest people's 17–20-point gains vanish: persons 2, 5 and 7 score 60.4, 66.7 and 68.1, back at their 2-s levels.
- **Late imagery adds nothing across people.** The extra 1.5 s at the end of the trial didn't help, which fits diagnostic 3: late imagery transfers poorly from person to person.
- **The arrow's last 0.75 s still counts.** This window includes it and scores 11.7 points above the window after the arrow (p = 0.020, higher for 8 of 9).
- Training fits as well as on our window (final loss 0.11–0.17), so the model isn't short of capacity or data here.

### Null check (4 Oct, 16:12): before the cue, chance
- **Window [cue − 0.5 s, cue]:** the fixation cross only. Nothing about the coming class exists yet, and the causal filters can't carry anything back from after the cue.
- **Riemannian models only** (4 min on the laptop, `neuros baseline --window -0.5 0`, code `66c4681`, clean):
  - across people: 47.4–51.3% for all six (TS+LR + EA 48.0 ± 2.3);
  - calibrated on the person's session 1: TS+LR 49.8 ± 3.2, MDM 50.4 ± 2.9;
  - every κ within ±0.05.
- **So the labels, epoch timing and alignment don't leak the class.** The deep runs share this data path, and the person-level splits are unit-tested against overlap.

## Caveats
- **One seed.** Deep-network results move by about a point between seeds. The post-cue drop (14.4 points, all 9 people) is far beyond that. More seeds belong on the cluster (Q6b).
- **9 people:** confidence intervals are ±5–7 points wide. These numbers check the pipeline; they prove nothing about 380 people.
- **The calibrated comparison is a simple Riemannian model** trained on one session (72 windows per class). A calibrated deep model might score higher, so the true gap may be larger than 11.6 points.
- **Offline, full-session alignment:** each test session's EA reference uses the whole session (unlabeled). The product gets a growing stream; E2 measures the cost.
- **Development, not headline:** BNCI2014-001 is a literature-comparison dataset used during development (D-026), never part of the locked test set.
