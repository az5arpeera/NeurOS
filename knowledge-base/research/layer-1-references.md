# Layer-1 references

Citations followed one layer out from the [primary papers](README.md), chosen because they bear on a decision we must make: alignment, UDA, contrastive/CLAP heads, datasets, and evaluation protocol. A few **adjacent** papers (not cited by the primary list, but directly on-question) are marked ➕.

**Read depth:** **F** = full text · **T** = targeted full text (method + results) · **A** = abstract only · **K** = well-known method, summarised from prior knowledge, not re-read this session. Don't quote a specific number from an **A** or **K** row without re-reading it.

## Alignment and adaptation (drives the UDA decision)

| Paper | Depth | Key facts | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| **He & Wu 2020**, "Transfer Learning for BCIs: A Euclidean Space Data Alignment Approach", IEEE TBME ([arXiv 1808.05464](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.05464)). Cited by: Revisiting EA, EAMCCA | A | The original EA: align trials in Euclidean space with R̄^(−1/2). Unsupervised, very cheap, works with any downstream model; beat Riemannian alignment on MI and ERP. | Foundation of our first pipeline stage |
| **Zanini et al. 2018**, "Transfer Learning: A Riemannian Geometry Framework with Applications to BCIs", IEEE TBME. Cited by: Revisiting EA | K | Riemannian re-centring (RA): move each domain's Riemannian mean covariance to I. Works on covariance matrices rather than raw trials. | RA slightly beats EA in the online adaptation results below |
| **Junqueira, Aristimunha, Chevallier, de Camargo 2024**, "A Systematic Evaluation of Euclidean Alignment with Deep Learning for EEG Decoding" ([arXiv 2401.10746](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.10746)). Cited by: Revisiting EA | T | BNCI2014-001 (left/right) and Schirrmeister2017 HGD (22-ch subset); EEGNet, ShallowNet, DeepNet. A shared model with EA gives **+4.33 points** on the target subject and **>70% faster convergence**. Pseudo-online EA used the **first 24 target trials** for the reference and cites a recommendation of **16–32 trials**. Individual-model ensembles with EA are +3.7 but still 3.62 below a shared model with EA. | **Kills the "3-second EA" claim.** A realistic passive warm-up is about 16–32 trials (1–3 min of use), or sliding windows of equivalent length |
| **Wimpff, Döbler, Yang 2024**, "Calibration-free online test-time adaptation for EEG motor imagery decoding", BCI Winter Conf. ([arXiv 2311.18520](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.18520), [code](https://github.com/martinwimpff/eeg-otta)). Cited by: Revisiting EA | T | **Source-free, online, no target data beforehand.** Cross-subject BCIC-IV-2a: source 57.38 → online EA 62.48 → online RA 63.64 → adaptive BN 64.32 → RA + BN 65.4–65.6 → **+ entropy minimisation (with label smoothing) 67.31%**. BCIC-IV-2b: 78.75 → **83.47%**. EMA and linear online updates match batch alignment. Buffer of 32 samples. | **The evidence-backed form of "UDA" for a live product**: about +10 points on 4-class with no calibration task |
| **Wimpff, Zerfowski, Yang 2025**, "Tailoring deep learning for real-time BCIs: from offline models to calibration-free online decoding" ([arXiv 2507.06779](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.06779)) | T | Real-time adaptive pooling (RAP) turns offline CNNs into efficient sliding-window decoders. Feedback updated every **62.5 ms**; BaseNet inference **2.15 ms**. Adds source-free domain adaptation across varying amounts of target data, and supports co-adaptive (user-learning) BCIs. | Blueprint for the live engine: latency budget, sliding windows, online adaptation |
| **Kobler, Hirayama, Zhao, Kawanabe 2022**, "SPD domain-specific batch normalization to crack interpretable unsupervised domain adaptation in EEG", NeurIPS ([arXiv 2206.01323](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.01323)) | A (PDF extracted, not yet mined) | SPDDSMBN in TSMNet: a **per-domain Riemannian batch norm**, i.e. EA/RA as a learnable layer with domain-specific statistics. Multi-source/multi-target and online UDA; 6 datasets; state of the art for inter-session and inter-subject transfer. | The in-network, per-user version of alignment. The top candidate if we move Riemannian math inside the model (v2) |
| **Brooks et al. 2019**, "Riemannian batch normalization for SPD neural networks", NeurIPS. Cited by: RUNet | K | Batch norm on the SPD manifold (Riemannian mean re-centring with running statistics). | What RUNet's re-centring layer implements |
| **Ganin et al. 2016**, "Domain-Adversarial Training of Neural Networks" (DANN), JMLR. Cited by: EA+SWA paper | K | A gradient reversal layer plus a domain classifier make features indistinguishable across domains. | The classic "adversarial UDA". Reported **unstable on small EEG data** (EA+SWA paper); treat as an experiment, not the core |
| **Sun & Saenko 2016**, "Deep CORAL" ➕ (named in the founder notes) | K | Loss that matches second-order feature statistics (covariances) between domains. | A cheap alternative to DANN for train-time domain generalisation |
| **Izmailov et al. 2018**, "Averaging Weights Leads to Wider Optima and Better Generalization" (SWA), UAI. Cited by: EA+SWA paper | K | Average weights along the late training trajectory, giving flatter minima and better generalisation. | A near-free training trick; include |

## Representations and backbones

| Paper | Depth | Key facts | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Huang & Van Gool 2017**, "A Riemannian Network for SPD Matrix Learning" (SPDNet), AAAI. Cited by: EEGNet+SPDNet | K | BiMap / ReEig / LogEig layers for learning directly on SPD matrices. | v2 encoder option |
| **Schirrmeister et al. 2017**, "Deep learning with convolutional neural networks for EEG decoding and visualization", HBM. Cited by: EEGNet, RUNet, EAMCCA, graph paper | K | ShallowConvNet and DeepConvNet; introduces the High-Gamma Dataset (HGD). | Standard baselines |
| **Chen et al. 2020**, SimCLR, ICML. Cited by: RUNet | K | Contrastive self-supervised learning with augmentations and the NT-Xent/InfoNCE loss. | RUNet's resting-state pretraining |
| **Wang et al. 2025**, CBraMod, ICLR. Cited by: INCEPT | K | Criss-cross Transformer EEG foundation model pretrained on TUEG. PhysioNet-MI 4-class fine-tuned 62.73% BA (as reported in INCEPT). | FM baseline |
| **Han, Wei & Faisal 2023**, "EEG decoding for datasets with heterogeneous electrode configurations using transfer learning graph neural networks", JNE. Cited by: graph paper | **not read, high priority** | GNN transfer across different electrode layouts. | Directly relevant to the "any headset" claim |
| **Chevallier et al. 2024**, "The largest EEG-based BCI reproducibility study for open science: the MOABB benchmark" ([arXiv 2404.15319](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15319)). Linked from the MOABB docs | A | 36 datasets, 30 pipelines (11 raw-signal, 13 Riemannian, 6 deep learning); MI (14 datasets), P300 (15), SSVEP (7). Riemannian pipelines are strongest, and deep learning needs a lot of data to compete. | Include Riemannian baselines (tangent space + logistic regression, MDM) in our benchmark; they're what reviewers expect |

## Contrastive / multimodal decoding (drives the CLAP decision)

| Paper | Depth | Key facts | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Radford et al. 2021**, CLIP. Cited by: ELIPformer | K | Contrastive image–text pretraining; zero-shot classification by text prompts. | The prior used by ELIPformer and NICE |
| **Elizalde et al. 2023** (MS-CLAP) / **Wu et al. 2023** (LAION-CLAP) ➕ | K | Contrastive language–audio pretraining; a shared audio–text embedding. | The "CLAP" in our plan (if audio) |
| **Huang et al. 2022**, MuLan; **Agostinelli et al. 2023**, MusicLM. Cited by: Brain2Music | K | Music–text joint embedding; text/embedding-conditioned music generation. | Brain2Music's target and generator |
| **Défossez, Caucheteux, Rapin, Kabeli, King 2023**, "Decoding speech perception from non-invasive brain recordings", *Nature Machine Intelligence* ([arXiv 2208.12266](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.12266)) ➕ | T | A CLIP-style contrastive loss from M/EEG to **wav2vec 2.0** speech embeddings; 4 public datasets, **175 participants**. Zero-shot identification of 3-s segments, top-10: **MEG up to 72.5% of 1,594**, **EEG up to 19.1% of 2,604**. Uses a **per-subject layer**, so subjects must appear in training. | The strongest non-invasive "brain → pretrained audio embedding" result. It shows EEG is far weaker than MEG, and that cross-subject needs subject handling |
| **Song et al. 2024**, NICE, "Decoding Natural Images from EEG for Object Recognition", ICLR ([arXiv 2308.13234](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.13234)) ➕ | T | THINGS-EEG2, **200-way zero-shot** image identification. Subject-dependent top-1 13.8% (NICE) / 15.6% (NICE-GA), top-5 39.5 / 42.8. **Subject-independent (leave-one-subject-out) top-1 6.2%, top-5 21.4%**. Chance 0.5% / 2.5%. | Cross-subject contrastive EEG decoding works, but at **~12× chance, not "read any thought"** |
| **Qing, Lu, Li 2026**, "Channel-Oriented Design for EEG-to-Music Reconstruction" ([arXiv 2606.04040](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04040)) ➕ | T (HTML) | EEG → **frozen CLAP audio encoder** with a CLIP-style loss, then AudioLDM. NMED-T (20 subjects, 10 songs) + NMED-H (48 subjects, 4 songs), 125 ch at 150 Hz, **29.4 h**. **Within-subject random 95/5 split** (no cross-subject test). 50-way identification **48.7%** (chance 2%); 14-way 69.2%; 10-way genre 20.3%. | The most direct precedent for "EEG → CLAP". **Nobody has shown it cross-subject.** NMED-T is fully crossed (every subject heard the same 10 songs), so a LOSO test is feasible |
| **Postolache et al. 2024**, "Naturalistic Music Decoding from EEG Data via Latent Diffusion Models", ICASSP 2025 ([arXiv 2405.09062](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.09062)) ➕ | A | Latent diffusion music reconstruction from raw NMED-T EEG; embedding-based metrics. | Context for EEG → music |
| **O'Sullivan et al. 2015**, auditory attention decoding, *Cerebral Cortex*. Cited by: EEG headphones | K | Linear stimulus-reconstruction decoder for the attended speech stream. | The baseline a CLAP-based attention decoder would beat |
| **Banville et al. 2025**, "Scaling laws for decoding images from brain activity" ➕ | **not read** | EEG/MEG/fMRI image-decoding scaling with data. | Needed if we ever claim "decoding improves with scale" |
