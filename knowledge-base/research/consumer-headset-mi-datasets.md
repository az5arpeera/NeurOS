# Public data covers half the hardware test

Public data lets NeurOS test its decoder on cheaper hardware worn by *different* people, but not on the same brain across two headsets. Of about a dozen public motor-imagery (MI) or motor-execution (ME) datasets recorded on consumer, low-cost, wireless or dry hardware, **five pass all three of NeurOS's filters** (a left-vs-right upper-limb task, at least two named sensorimotor channels, a licence that allows commercial use): **MILimbEEG** (OpenBCI, 16 dry electrodes, 60 people), **Liu2024** (ZhenTec saline-sponge headset, 50 acute-stroke patients), **UET175** (Emotiv EPOC Flex saline, 30 stroke patients), and two sets on g.tec's wireless but research-grade g.Nautilus, **Leeuwis2021** (wet, 55 people) and **Shin2022** (dry, 10 people). **No public dataset records the same people doing left/right imagery on both research-grade wet and consumer or dry hardware**; the closest, Rokai et al. 2026, has 16 people doing motor *execution* on a semi-dry MindRove with C3/Cz/C4 and on a wet mBrainTrain SMARTING, and carries no licence. The headsets customers most likely own are the worst covered: Muse and Emotiv Insight have no sensorimotor channel, the EPOC family reaches only FC5/FC6, and the Unicorn and Neurosity Crown have the right layout but no labelled public MI data. Published comparisons put the hardware penalty at **0-2 points when the same people are tested** and **about 5-10 points when people, setting or channel count also change**, while crossing datasets without alignment falls to near chance; nobody has published a zero-calibration cross-subject result on consumer MI data, so NeurOS's would be the first. **Download MILimbEEG, Liu2024 and Leeuwis2021 first** (all CC BY 4.0, all in MOABB, all with C3/C4), then UET175, and have the founders ask the Rokai authors for licence terms, because theirs is the only public route to a same-person test. Most candidates keep the class cue on screen during imagery, the confound that inflated NeurOS's E0 result, so every run needs NeurOS's post-cue windows and cue-only control.

## The headsets with the right layout have no labelled public data

Public MI data from cheap hardware is a thin, recent layer. A 2023 review of 25 public MI/ME datasets found all of them recorded on research-grade systems such as BrainAmp, SynAmps, g.USBamp, BioSemi and Neurofax ([Gwon et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10101208/)). The consumer and low-cost sets that have appeared since cluster in two device families.

**OpenBCI** has three relevant sets: a gel-cap MI-vs-rest set (Peterson2020), a 16-electrode dry set (MILimbEEG) and an 8-channel dry Ultracortex set from the Rafique group, with an unlicensed GitHub set as a fourth. **Emotiv** has the most, spread across its product line: MIMED2024 on the EPOC X, MartinezPeon2024 on the EPOC, MIBMPI2024 and UET175 on the EPOC Flex, Fernandez-Fraga 2019, DICIS and Rafique on the EPOC+, and one IEEE DataPort set on the Insight. Single datasets exist for the CGX Quick-20m dry cap (MartinezPeon2025), the Neuroelectrics Enobio 32 dry (MOVING2024), the ZhenTec NT1 saline-sponge headset (Liu2024) and the EEG-Hero dry headset (BNCI 001-2020). Leeuwis2021 and Shin2022 run on g.tec's g.Nautilus, a wireless but research-grade amplifier, so they test form factor and electrode contact more than consumer electronics ([MOABB dataset summary](https://moabb.neurotechx.com/docs/dataset_summary.html)).

**Channel layout rules out most of the consumer catalogue.** Muse records at TP9, AF7, AF8 and TP10 ([GitHub vinayakr99](https://github.com/vinayakr99/Muse-MotorImageryClassification)). Emotiv Insight records at AF3, AF4, T7, T8 and Pz ([Emotiv Insight 2 specifications](https://emotiv.gitbook.io/insight-2-user-manual/introduction/technical-specifications)). NeuroSky uses one forehead electrode ([PMC10181463](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10181463/)), so none of the three has a sensorimotor site. The EPOC, EPOC+ and EPOC X get no closer than FC5/FC6 ([EPOC X manual](https://emotiv.gitbook.io/epoc-x-user-manual/introduction/technical-specifications)), which means their datasets test montage transfer rather than decoding from the hand area.

The two consumer headsets whose layouts suit NeurOS are the g.tec Unicorn (C3, Cz and C4 among its 8 channels) and the Neurosity Crown (CP3, C3, C4, CP4). **Neither has labelled public MI data.** MOABB's only Unicorn dataset is an ERP set ([MOABB dataset summary](https://moabb.neurotechx.com/docs/dataset_summary.html)); the 5-person Unicorn Hybrid Black MI set behind TFOC-Net is only promised, "The recorded EEG dataset will be made publicly available" ([arXiv 2507.02510](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.02510)), and a two-Unicorn, 16-channel study did not release its data ([arXiv 2412.08971](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.08971v1)). The Crown has a 133-session Crown 3 dump with 8 channels at 256 Hz, in CSV under an MIT licence, but its task labels are not documented ([GitHub JeremyNixon/neurosity](https://github.com/JeremyNixon/neurosity)). NeurOS already has a Crown montage file for its virtual-headset work, so this dump is worth one afternoon of inspection. The largest dry MI collection of all, 530 exhibition visitors on a 19-electrode dry Wearable Sensing DSI-24 with C3/C4 ([Essex accepted manuscript](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/42395/1/JNE_109459_Final.pdf)), "cannot be made publicly available" and is offered only on reasonable request ([IOP](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1741-2552/ae2e8a)).

Searches found no MI dataset for Bitbrain, BrainBit, IDUN, Galea, Ganglion, Mentalab Explore or BrainAccess. mBrainTrain's only public set is imagined speech under CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 ([MOABB AguileraRodriguez2025](https://moabb.neurotechx.com/docs/generated/moabb.datasets.AguileraRodriguez2025.html)).

Applying NeurOS's three filters (left/right upper limb, at least two named sensorimotor channels, commercial licence) leaves **MILimbEEG, Liu2024, UET175, Leeuwis2021 and Shin2022**. Only MILimbEEG combines consumer hardware with healthy participants, and Shin2022 passes only if MOABB's guess about its unlabelled channels holds. The Rafique OpenBCI Ultracortex set (CC0, 7 people) could become a sixth once its electrode positions are checked.

Three practical frictions follow from these tables. **Sampling rates are low but adequate**: OpenBCI-with-Daisy data are 125 Hz and Emotiv data 128 Hz, so both need only light resampling into NeurOS's 128 Hz contract, the 8-30 Hz band sits well below Nyquist, and MILimbEEG's factory 5-50 Hz filtering suits an 8-30 Hz pipeline. **Trial counts strain per-session Euclidean alignment.** Liu2024 has 40 trials per person ([PMC10811218](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10811218/)), while UET175 averages about 12 trials per session (2,640 / 220) ([PMC12209182](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12209182/)), below the 16-32 trials NeurOS's own knowledge base cites for an alignment warm-up, so alignment there will likely have to pool a person's sessions. MILimbEEG has only about five repetitions per class per run ([PMC10505670](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10505670/)); its unlabelled windows from the other tasks in the same run could still feed alignment, but that is an inference to test, not a reported result. **Shin2022's channels are unlabelled**, and NeurOS's layout check needs named sites, so MOABB's EEG7/EEG9-as-C3/C4 mapping would enter the pipeline as an untested assumption ([MOABB Shin2022](https://moabb.neurotechx.com/docs/generated/moabb.datasets.Shin2022.html)).

## No public dataset records the same brains imagining movement on two headsets

Searches of Zenodo, Figshare, OSF, Mendeley, IEEE DataPort, OpenNeuro, Hugging Face and MOABB found **no public dataset in which the same people perform left/right-hand MI on both a research-grade wet system and a consumer or dry system**. **Rokai 2026 is the only public dataset that comes close to the test NeurOS wants.** The same 16 people did a motor task on a semi-dry consumer-style headset with C3/Cz/C4 and on a wet research-grade system, but the task is execution rather than imagery and the repository has no licence, which by default means all rights reserved. The Lee 2026, Zhu 2021 and Lee 2021 sets are openly licensed and genuinely same-person, yet none can test MI decoding. What they can test is the signal layer: whether NeurOS's normalisation, alignment and artifact handling make one brain look alike across headsets. Lee 2026 already reports alpha-peak differences of only 0.20-0.32 Hz between each consumer device and the DSI-24, none significant ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-026-06962-5)), though forehead, ear and occipital layouts limit how far such results carry to a motor strip.

Several studies recorded exactly the right pairing but kept the data. **Jeong 2021** recorded the same 10 people doing lower-limb MI on wet actiCAP Slim and dry actiCAP Xpress Twist electrodes over 31 channels including C3/C4/Cz, with no data statement ([PMC8513081](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8513081/)). **Kleeva 2024** recorded 13 people at rest on dry PSBD Headphones with **C3/C4**, on Muse and on a gel NVX system; its data "will be made available by the authors, without undue reservation", but no URL is given ([PMC10873917](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10873917/)). Ehrhardt 2024 recorded 33 people on wet and dry 64-channel ANT systems and restricts sharing under GDPR to requests ([PMC11576458](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11576458/)), while Hinrichs 2020 ([PMC7090045](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7090045/)) and Kam 2019 ([eScholarship](https://escholarship.org/content/qt4jb093sp/qt4jb093sp_noSplash_dc1356855d085fe12bc7b047a4bbfaf2.pdf)) carry no data statement at all.

Two widely cited "cross-hardware" resources are not same-person. Schwarz 2020 (BNCI 001-2020) ran its gel, water and dry systems "on an independent group of 15 study participants" each ([PMC7438923](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7438923/)). **Hassan 2026's Zenodo "cross-hardware" benchmark** merges five wet research MI datasets (PhysioNet MI, Cho2017, Lee2019-MI, Shin2017A, Weibo2014), harmonised to 20 channels, 8-32 Hz and 100 Hz with Euclidean Alignment already applied ([Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/20727886)). It tests the leave-one-dataset-out generalisation that NeurOS's E1 already runs, and it republishes under CC BY 4.0 data whose sources include GPL-3.0 (Shin2017A) and ODC-By (PhysioNet) terms ([MOABB summary](https://moabb.neurotechx.com/docs/dataset_summary.html)), so commercial work should rebuild from the originals.

## Same-person hardware gaps are small; dataset shifts are not

Three studies compare hardware on the same people in a motor paradigm, and two of them find almost no gap. In Jeong 2021, ten people doing three-class lower-limb imagery scored **58.44% on dry electrodes versus 58.66% on wet** ([PMC8513081](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8513081/)). In Rokai 2026, sixteen people doing motor execution scored **78.8% on the semi-dry MindRove versus 80.9% on the wet SMARTING**, "the difference was not significant", and the MindRove even showed higher SNR for VEP (11.26 vs 9.26 dB) and P300 (13.01 vs 10.17 dB) ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-52672-8)). The outlier is also the oldest: with only six dry electrodes, Popescu 2007's five people transferred information "on average about 30% slower than the standard cap" ([EuropePMC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=TITLE:%22Single%20trial%20classification%20of%20motor%20imagination%20using%206%20dry%20EEG%20electrodes%22&resultType=core&format=json)), a result that reflects a six-electrode setup as much as dry contact. Peterson 2020 ran the same MI-vs-rest protocol on OpenBCI with gel and on a 64-channel ANT Neuro system and found an OpenBCI deficit of **4.0 points with CSP (73.15% vs 77.12%), shrinking to about 1 point with the best pipeline (83.80% vs 84.84%)** ([PMC7056654](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7056654/)); the notes do not say whether the same people sat for both.

**Gaps widen when the people, setting or channel count change too.** Schwarz 2020 put three separate groups of 15 through three-class reach-and-grasp and found dry 56.4%, gel 61.3% and water-based 62.3% against a 42.8% chance level, with dry significantly lower than both ([PMC7438923](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7438923/)). NeuroPath pre-trained on medical-grade data and fine-tuned within-subject on a self-collected saline Emotiv FLEX 2 set, reaching 73.9% on four-class MI at 32 channels, 72.8% at 16 and 69.2% at 8, against 83.7% for the same model on BCI Competition IV-2a ([arXiv 2604.09654](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.09654)); that roughly 10-point gap mixes hardware, people and protocol. Sultana 2025 tested 530 exhibition visitors on a dry DSI-24: **61% of users were above chance, against 80% on a wet g.USBamp in the lab (p=0.0015) but 65% on a wet BrainAmp (p=0.49, not significant)** ([Essex accepted manuscript](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/42395/1/JNE_109459_Final.pdf)). Its signal-quality analysis found the dry system robust in moderate noise, with more muscle and broadband artifact in dynamic conditions but similar brain-component proportions across systems ([Essex repository](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/42395/)). The BrainAmp comparison is the instructive one: dry electrodes in a noisy hall did no worse than one wet lab system, so the 19-point gap against g.USBamp reflects setting and amplifier at least as much as electrode contact.

**Crossing datasets costs far more than changing electrodes.** Xu 2020 reduced eight MI datasets to C3/Cz/C4 and found within-dataset cross-subject accuracies of 51-89%, yet "most cross-dataset accuracies of ShallowNet without OPS were near the random level" ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2020.00103/full)). Unsupervised alignment narrows but does not close the gap: SDDA's transfer from 22-channel BCI IV-2a to 3-channel IV-2b reached 75.10%, against 72.49% for the common-channel baseline ([arXiv 2503.05349](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.05349)). The one direct wet-to-dry transfer result, ALPHA, used each person's wet-electrode EEG to improve their dry-electrode decoder enough to beat a fully calibrated TRCA ([EuropePMC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:34406934&resultType=core&format=json)), but it worked on SSVEP, which is stimulus-locked and far easier to align than imagery.

Within-subject numbers on consumer hardware scatter widely, which is itself a warning about lab-to-field transfer. A 2025 review lists an 8-channel Unicorn Hybrid Black at 35.9 ± 10.4% online on four classes, a 16-channel Enobio at 87.37% on two classes and BlueBCI at 71.19% on two ([PMC12389868](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12389868/)). Two Unicorn Naked headsets reached 78% on a single day, 75% over three days and 62% in real-world robot control ([arXiv 2412.08971](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.08971v1)); an 8-channel dry InMex system scored 52.78% offline and 47.42% online, rising to 66.20% after continual adaptation ([PMC13203728](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13203728/)). Among the ranked datasets, Liu2024's own left/right baselines ran from 55.57% (CSP+LDA) to 72.21% (the authors' method) ([PMC10811218](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10811218/)), and Leeuwis2021's online CSP+LDA error was 25.83% ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2021.634748/full)). High numbers from headsets without sensorimotor channels deserve suspicion: a Muse 2 GitHub project reports 83.8% left/right ([GitHub](https://github.com/vinayakr99/Muse-MotorImageryClassification)), and a single forehead NeuroSky electrode matched a 16-channel V-AMP within one recording collection but "dropped considerably" across collections ([PMC10181463](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10181463/)). By contrast, an EPOC X without C3/C4 decoded six states at 0.17-0.36 accuracy with kappa near or below zero ([PMC12378764](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12378764/)), which is the result one would expect when no motor-cortex signal is present.

**No published study trains an MI decoder on wet research-grade EEG and tests it without calibration on dry or consumer hardware**, whether on the same people or not. No cross-subject result was found for any public consumer MI dataset. The same-person evidence predicts a small hardware penalty, and the cross-dataset evidence predicts that changes in people and protocol will dominate. A NeurOS result on MILimbEEG or Liu2024 will measure both at once and cannot separate them.

## Download MILimbEEG, Liu2024 and Leeuwis2021 first

The ranking weighs how close the hardware is to what customers own, whether the task is left vs right upper limb, whether C3/C4-area channels are named, whether a commercial licence is confirmed at the source, whether a cue-free window exists, the number of people and trials, and the effort of writing a loader. Rokai 2026 sits outside the ranking: if its authors grant terms, it becomes the most valuable dataset on the list, because it alone isolates hardware from people.

| Rank | Dataset | Why it ranks here | Main caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **MILimbEEG** | Real consumer hardware (OpenBCI, NeurOS's first-customer platform) with dry electrodes; 60 people; C3/C4 plus FC/CP neighbours; left/right hand MI; CC BY; MOABB loader | About 5 trials per class per run; cue persistence unknown; channel names only from the README; participant pool includes amputees and post-COVID cases |
| 2 | **Liu2024** | Wireless semi-dry saline; 9 central/fronto-central/centro-parietal sites; left vs right grasp; 50 people × 40 trials; CC BY; BIDS; MOABB | Acute stroke adds a pathology shift; a grip video plays during imagery |
| 3 | **Leeuwis2021** | 55 healthy novices; left vs right; 16 channels with C3/Cz/C4; arrow gone after 1.25 s; MOABB | Research-grade amplifier (tests few-channel wireless, not consumer); online feedback during the task; licence only from MOABB |
| 4 | **UET175** | Emotiv EPOC Flex, a consumer brand; 22 motor-area channels; 30 people over 220 sessions; CC BY; 434 MB | Stroke; arm lift rather than hand; prompt stays on screen; about 12 trials per session; no MOABB loader |
| 5 | **Shin2022** | Dry g.SAHARA electrodes; left vs right; MOABB | 10 people; unlabelled channels; continuous cursor feedback |
| 6 | **Peterson2020** | OpenBCI with gel; CC0 data record; published OpenBCI-vs-ANT comparison | MI vs rest only, and the arrow appears only on MI trials, a direct visual confound |
| 7 | **MIMED2024** | 30 people × 2 days; left and right hand; CC BY | FC5/FC6 only, so it is a montage stress test, not a motor-cortex test |
| 8 | **Neurosity Crown dump** | Real consumer headset with C3/C4/CP3/CP4; MIT | No documented labels; may be unusable |
| 9 | **Rafique Ultracortex** | Dry OpenBCI, left/right hand, CC0 | 7 people; positions and timing unknown |
| 10 | **MOVING2024, BNCI 001-2020** | Dry, motor-cortex coverage; useful for movement-vs-rest | Right hand only (MOVING, cue on screen); grasp types (BNCI) |

**The first wave should be MILimbEEG, Liu2024 and Leeuwis2021.** All three have left-vs-right tasks over C3/C4, CC BY 4.0 licences and existing MOABB loaders. Between them they cover three distinct hardware shifts: consumer dry, semi-dry saline and few-channel wireless. Their sizes were not found, so each download request will need a size check first, under NeurOS's download rule. Before any training-adjacent use, verify Leeuwis2021's licence at DataverseNL, because NeurOS has already caught MOABB listing a wrong licence for another dataset. Shin2022 is a cheap fourth if its size is small, but only after deciding how to map its unlabelled channels.

**The second wave is UET175, Peterson2020 and MIMED2024.** UET175 is the only Emotiv set with real motor-cortex coverage and justifies a custom loader for its 434 MB of CSV. Peterson2020 supports an MI-vs-rest (idle-state) check, provided the arrow-only-on-MI confound is controlled. MIMED2024 shows how the decoder behaves when only FC5/FC6 are available, which is the situation most Emotiv owners are in. **Two datasets need inspection before any download request**: the Crown dump's labels decide whether it is noise or the only real Crown MI data, and the Ultracortex set's electrode positions decide whether it joins the first tier.

**Outreach can do what downloads cannot.** The founders should ask the Rokai authors for licence terms on the MindRove/SMARTING data, ask the Jeong 2021 and Kleeva 2024 authors for their same-person wet/dry recordings, ask the Sultana 2025 team for the 530-person DSI-24 set they offer on reasonable request, and ask the TFOC-Net authors about their promised Unicorn MI release. Any one of these would supply evidence that no public dataset now provides.

Every run on these datasets should follow NeurOS's existing cue-window rule (windows start 0.25 s after the class leaves the screen, or are flagged not cue-free) and include the cue-only control. Each transfer result should be reported next to a within-person calibrated baseline on the same device, as the notes recommend. Unlabelled-target alignment should be described as such, so the "zero-calibration" claim stays accurate. For scale, NeurOS's own cue-free E1 results on wet lab data were 69.4% pooled on BNCI2014-001 and 58.8% on Yang2025 (internal results). A consumer result within a few points of those would match the same-person literature.

## Thin spots: MOABB-only licences, missing sizes and conflicting notes

One of the two note files was saved by a researcher cut off by a usage limit and may be interim. Neither file is marked, so this report treats any fact found in only one note, and every disagreement between them, as provisional. The cross-device note looks like the less complete of the two: it reports no public CGX or Enobio MI dataset although MartinezPeon2025 and MOVING2024 are both documented in MOABB, it could not confirm MILimbEEG's channel names, and it did not identify the dry device in the 530-person study, all of which the consumer-device note found and cited. Where the notes disagree, this report follows the one that quotes a primary source.

**Six conflicts remain open.** The Peterson2020 arrow is quoted as "presented only for MI trials" ([PMC9114495](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9114495)), but one note says, without a quote, that it stays for the full 4 s. For Leeuwis2021, one note says wet gel and the other says the electrode type is unstated, and the paper's 1.25 s arrow contradicts MOABB's statement that the cue stays on screen. MILimbEEG's paper describes mostly one run per person while its README says ten sessions, and its file counts differ (7,440 versus over 8,680). MOVING2024's sampling rate is 256 Hz in the paper and 500 Hz in MOABB. One secondary summary gave Peterson2020 12 subjects against the primary sources' 10. And two datasets carry different licences on the article and the data record: Peterson2020 (CC0 versus CC BY-NC-ND) and Lee 2026 (CC BY 4.0 versus CC BY-NC-ND 4.0). The repository record normally governs the files, but that is a legal call to confirm.

**Several areas are thin rather than contradictory.** Licences for Leeuwis2021, Shin2022, MOVING2024 and MartinezPeon2024/2025 come only from MOABB pages, and those for BNCI 001-2020 (page returned 403), Fernandez-Fraga's UCI record and the Kaggle Muse set were not confirmed. Download sizes are missing for nearly every dataset except UET175, MIBMPI2024, the Insight set and the same-person sets. Cue persistence is unknown for MILimbEEG, MIMED2024, MartinezPeon2024/2025, MIBMPI2024 and both Rafique sets, and whether Liu2024's grip video differs by hand was not extracted. Channel positions are unknown for the Rafique Ultracortex and Hanafy sets, and MIBMPI2024's EPOC Flex sites are unverified. Accuracies were not extracted for Shin2022 or MIBMPI2024, nor the protocol behind MILimbEEG's roughly 74% figures (which appear to be execution, not imagery), nor MOABB benchmark results for the consumer sets, ALPHA's exact numbers or Martinez-Leon 2016's EPOC-versus-BioSemi comparison. A 99.36% figure for MIMED2024 could not be traced to a source and was excluded. NeuroPath's abstract calls its consumer data "public" while its full text describes self-collected data with no availability statement ([arXiv 2604.09654](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.09654)). WRCC2023 (a draft MOABB loader) and a 20-person, 7-day IEEE DataPort MI set state neither device nor licence. Finally, Kaggle was not searched exhaustively, BEETL and EEG foundation-model device-agnostic results were not reviewed, and a 2023 PLOS One scoping review of consumer EEG was not read, so a few more datasets may exist under non-obvious titles.

## Conclusion

The hardware-transfer question splits into two tests, and public data supports only the first. "Does a wet-trained decoder work for a new person on a cheap headset?" can be answered this month from MILimbEEG, Liu2024 and Leeuwis2021. "Does the headset itself cost accuracy for the same person?" cannot be answered from any public MI dataset. The same-person motor studies that exist say that second cost is small: 0-2 points, with semi-dry SNR sometimes better than wet. If NeurOS sees a large drop on MILimbEEG, the likelier culprits are the new people, the protocol and the cue, not the OpenBCI board. That changes the story NeurOS can tell. The commercial risk is the unseen user more than the cheap headset, and the evidence to prove it is a small consented recording of the same people on wet and consumer hardware.

The second implication is defensive. Most consumer MI datasets leave the cue on screen, and the Muse and NeuroSky literature shows how easily cue, eye or muscle signals pass for imagery. The first cross-subject consumer numbers NeurOS publishes will also be the first anyone can check. Pairing every one with a cue-only control and a per-device calibrated baseline is cheap now and would be hard to retrofit after the YC application.
